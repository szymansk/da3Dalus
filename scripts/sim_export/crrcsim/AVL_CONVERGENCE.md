### Diagnosis

The non-convergence of the tables above is a strip-alignment defect between wing and horizontal tail, not
physics and not the fin. With aligned strips the full airplane converges: NP 38.1 / 38.1 / 37.8 % MAC for
5/12, 10/24, 20/48 (core 0.25; |dNP| of the last two levels 0.3 % MAC, below the 0.5 tolerance) and e falls
1.020 / 0.997 / 0.989, below 1. Removing the fin changes nothing (identical to the digit: its trailing legs
do not touch the longitudinal case), shifting the tail by +-0.05 m moves NP by only +1.3 / +0.5 % MAC, and
the wing alone gives e 1.032 / 1.014 / 1.006 (converging to about 1; AVL's slight overshoot). The remaining
sensitivity is the vortex-core ratio: 0.5 instead of 0.25 moves NP by +2.3 % MAC at every level (a model
parameter of AVL's finite core, not a mesh effect); the AVL default 0.25 is used for the main results.
Result for BRYAN at the plan CG 31.4 % MAC: NP 37.8 % MAC, static margin 6.4 % (core 0.25), 8.6 % (core 0.5).

### Previous NP worlds

The AVL value in the NP worlds [32.9; 36.9] % MAC (NP_NIEDRIGE_RE.md, ERGEBNISSE_STABILITAET.md) came from
bryan_lateral_avl.py / bryan_np_claf.py: per-section spanwise_resolution=3 uniform, chordwise 10, no
alignment. That is the unaligned per-segment mesh of the first table (segment-3: NP 33.1 % MAC), which is
mesh-dependent (segment-1 37.5, segment-2 35.4, segment-3 33.1); the 32.9 % AVL world is therefore suspect and
low by about 5 % MAC. The upper bound 36.9 % comes from the textbook method (eta 0.9), not from AVL.
urmodell/fleet_eval.py used 4 strips per segment (uniform), chordwise 8: the same class of mesh.
